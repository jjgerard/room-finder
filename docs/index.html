One room per class, no clashes, nothing moved off campus

Spring 2026 at Belfast splits hundreds of classes across two or more rooms. This is a rebuilt timetable that gives every class a single room — exams excepted, since a sitting may legitimately fill several — breaks none of the hard rules, and improves both soft goals on top — while leaving most of the timetable where it is.

A changed room is invisible to students beyond the room number on their timetable. A changed time or day is what people actually notice, which is why the search tries every room swap before it moves anything in time.

The soft goals, which also improved

Fitting the hard rules did not come at the cost of the week students actually experience. Both soft goals are better than the timetable running today.

A gap day is a day with no classes sitting between two days that have them — a cohort taught Mon/Wed/Fri has two; one taught Mon/Tue/Wed has none. Wednesday afternoons were not optimised for, and did get slightly busier.

Two findings worth knowing

Back-to-back was said to be impossible. It isn't.

The earlier analysis concluded that about 27 modules could never have their lecture and seminar back-to-back, because no free four-hour window existed on any day. It reached 95 of 136 pairs and recommended falling back to "same day, with a gap".

All 146 linked groups here are strictly back-to-back, in their current order. The difference is not a better search — it is that contiguity is built into how a group is represented, so it is never something the search has to repair and then protect.

No block teaching had to go off campus.

The earlier analysis found three all-day sessions (NUS762, NUS767, BUS506) that had to move offsite to reach a clean solution.

All 327 block-teaching sessions stay on campus here, those three included. Belfast's rooms are empty enough to absorb them once the classes around them are free to shuffle.

Before you rely on this

The clash data is inferred, not authoritative. "These two classes share students" was derived from the current timetable — same programme and year for a student clash, and same school plus shared room plus never currently overlapping as a proxy for the same lecturer. It will both over-constrain (two cohorts that never really meet) and under-constrain (a genuinely shared lecturer the proxy missed). Re-run this against real enrolment and staff-assignment data before acting on it.

Class sizes are room capacities, not headcounts. Every "this class fits that room" judgement inherits that. A class whose current room is generous looks bigger than it is, and one in a room that is too small looks smaller.

Most specialist rooms have no recorded capacity. Those rooms are recorded as capacity 0, which means "unknown", not "no seats". Because of that a class already sitting in one is allowed to stay there, and capacity is only enforced on a class that moves. Tighten this once real capacities exist.

Exams may use several rooms; the exam set-up block is left alone. An exam is not a normal class, so the one-room rule does not apply to it — each multi-room exam keeps the number of rooms it uses today, in distinct rooms at one slot. Ten rows marked "Do NOT Edit or Remove booking" reserving rooms for the exam weeks are pinned and never moved; only their dominant room is in the data, so weeks 15–16 availability is optimistic.

One-off room bookings were excluded. The source data carries 540 BK rows — a named person booking a room on a specific date, with no module, no cohort and no place in the clash graph. They were specific to spring 2026 and are not rescheduled, so they are not part of this timetable and their rooms are not reserved in it.
