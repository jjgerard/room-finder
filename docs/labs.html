One room

Eight computer labs on the Belfast campus carry CEBE in their names, six of them School of Computing. Eight schools teach in them. This asks what it would cost to take the name literally — and finds that seven of the eight could be reserved tomorrow with nobody noticing, while the eighth is the difference between a legal spring timetable and none.

The eight rooms

Demand counts classes that do not already teach in a CEBE lab but could use this one. Sole option counts those for which it is the only CEBE room that fits them at all.

Why only one of them counts

What the ring-fence costs

Three arms, twenty random starting points each, on Spring 2026 — the denser of the two terms and the one with least slack. Everything but the room rule is identical between arms, so the difference is the ring-fence and nothing else.

What this means

Seven of the eight labs could be reserved for the School of Computing with no effect on anyone. The eighth, BC-03-311, is the only computer lab on campus holding more than 50 — and in the tighter of the two terms it is the difference between a timetable that breaks no rules and one that does. The ask is one room, not eight.

How this was checked, and where it is soft

Three things that would weaken it, stated rather than waited for
  • Nobody is stranded. Not one of the classes that loses its only CEBE option is left with nowhere at all — they have rooms outside the set. The cost is lost flexibility, not impossibility, which is a weaker claim than it first sounds and the true one.
  • Spring has little slack anyway. It reaches a legal timetable from 6 of 30 starting points against autumn's 9, and it is the denser term — 3,302 bookings against 2,369. A term that tight will tip under most added restrictions. Autumn absorbs the same ring-fence without becoming unsolvable, and that is the honest limit on the claim.
  • Candidate rooms are partly inferred. Which rooms a class may use is derived from the model, and 98% of class sizes in it are inferred from the room the class currently sits in rather than from a confirmed figure. So "rooms available" is the pool the model considers eligible, not a survey of what would physically work.
Two ways this analysis was wrong before it was right
  • It ring-fenced the catering kitchens. The first room pattern was /CEBE|MARCS|CAD/, which matches BD ACADemy on the substring. That run produced a confident answer to a question nobody had asked. The pattern now uses word boundaries, and a test checks the eight rooms matched are the eight intended.
  • It ran at five seeds, and measured noise. At five starting points the variance between runs is larger than the difference between configurations, and that version scored some room releases as worse than fencing everything — which cannot happen, since releasing a room only ever adds options. That impossibility is the only thing that revealed it. The arms below are compared on median and mean as well as best, at twenty seeds.
